PiMap

Every resident reaches safety, whether or not they can walk.

PiMap shows your nearest shelter and the safest way there, every day and in an emergency. It connects residents, neighbours and the city on one map.

A prototype built for City Resilience Hack 2026 in Tallinn. Not an official City of Tallinn service.

The problem

Cities have shelters, sirens and emergency alerts, but no plan for the last kilometre. If you cannot walk it, you do not reach it.

Older people, people with limited mobility and families with small children are the first to be left behind.

How PiMap works

1

Map

One alert brings your nearest shelter, a route that avoids danger, and four ways to get there: walk, bike, drive or request a pick-up. It works without signal.

2

Unlock

During an alert, shared scooters and bikes near shelters become free to ride, time-boxed and limited to the shelter area.

3

Lend

Neighbours pre-list spare bikes and mobility aids and offer seats in their cars. People who cannot walk are picked up first.

A protocol for each emergency

War or air strike

Routes to the nearest public shelter.

Flood

The city marks flooded streets and routes go around them.

Heatwave

Routes to cool indoor places along the shadiest streets.

Storm

Routes avoid trees, the shoreline and reported fallen trees.

Useful every day

  • Find the nearest pharmacy, hospital, grocery store or drinking water.
  • See construction works and routes that steer around them, kept up to date by the city.
  • Report issues and share ideas for your street. Neighbours support them and the city replies.

Two portals, one map

Residents use the citizen portal. City staff use the municipality portal to update construction, declare and run emergency protocols, and answer residents.

Open data

  • Public shelters: the Estonian Rescue Board register, through the Land and Spatial Development Board geoportal.
  • Places, buildings, trees and walking routes: OpenStreetMap contributors.

Who is building it

PiMap is built by Pinge Electronics OÜ in Tallinn, which runs light electric vehicles and mobility aids for people who find walking hard.

The next step is a one-district pilot with the city and the Rescue Board. The code is open.
